Small conversations can open a door.

Talking openly about suicide does not put the idea in someone's head. WHO recommends listening, asking directly when you are concerned, and encouraging professional support. Learn more from WHO.

💬

Ask and listen

If you're worried about someone, choose a quiet moment and ask how they're doing. Listen without judgement.

🤝

Stay connected

Let them know you care. If they're in immediate danger, don't leave them alone and seek urgent help.

🫶

Connect to support

Encourage contact with a doctor, mental-health professional, counsellor, crisis line, or trusted person.

Need support right now?

If you are thinking about suicide or self-harm, please reach out to someone you trust and seek professional help. If you are in immediate danger, contact local emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department.

If you're worried about someone.

WHO identifies signs such as major mood changes, withdrawal, talking about ending one's life, saying goodbye, or giving away valued possessions as reasons to take concern seriously. These signs do not predict who will die by suicide; they are reasons to reach out and seek help. Source: WHO.

1. Be present

Choose a calm place, listen with empathy, and let the person know you are there.

2. Ask directly

When you're concerned, asking directly about suicide does not cause someone to attempt suicide.

3. Get help

Encourage professional support. If danger is immediate, contact emergency services and don't leave the person alone.
